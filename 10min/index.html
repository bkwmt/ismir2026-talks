15 s Good evening, and thank you. This is my first ISMIR, and it is an honour to speak in this session. This talk is about the people behind the sound of postwar Taiwanese popular music.

42 s Consider a listener in a restaurant who hears an unfamiliar pop song and cannot name the song or the singer. From the sound alone, many listeners can still place it in a decade, because they hear how the sound has changed. Some, especially those who play an instrument, go further and recognise whose style it is: a particular player, or a particular arranger. Underneath genre and style, in other words, are the people who made the sound. These are the arrangers, the players and the engineers who stand behind the singer, and we call them the backstage.

36 s Let us play with one word, meta. If backstage credits enter MIR at all, they enter as metadata: data about a recording. The people behind them then become what we call meta-persons, the persons behind the person in front. One step further, we reach metaphysics, which reaches beyond the physical to the question of being. Our question, then, is whether these people exist in MIR data at all. That is what our title means by digital existence: it is first a claim about existence.

38 s Our framing starts from Georgina Born's critique of MIR, published in 2020. For Born, datasets and benchmarks encode what music is and who makes it, and what MIR calls an ontology, a set of classes, attributes and relations, would be identified in musicology as > "the analysis and modelling of an epistemology of music, that is, conceptual knowledge about a certain music." In philosophy, ontology concerns what exists, while epistemology concerns how we know it, so in Born's reading MIR uses the first word for the second.

45 s For Born, then, MIR's problem is reduction: a rich musical reality, with its bodies, places and social relations, is flattened into classes. The humanities have a similar blind spot: histories of popular music seldom discuss the backstage, who actually produced the music. In postwar Taiwan, moreover, there was nothing to reduce, because no database had ever recorded them. Their problem is absence, and today absence from metadata amounts to erasure from the historical record. We therefore take Born's warning seriously, but argue for a reversal: for the backstage, encoding, that is, entering them into a database with their roles and ties, is an ontological intervention.

28 s Our second step rests on a hypothesis about how this music was made. We assume that people who often work together come to sound alike. A collaboration network can tell us who worked with whom, and the sound can then test whether they do sound alike. If the two agree, the ties that the network reconstructs have also left a trace in the recordings themselves.

35 s To test this, we built two digital objects and kept them strictly independent. The first is a collaboration network built from album credits, and it formalises who worked with whom. The second is an acoustic embedding space built from recordings, and it formalises, as far as a model can, what listeners recognise: the work itself, credited or not. Because neither sees the other's data, if both objects locate the same structure, that structure is unlikely to be an artefact of either.

34 s The network comes from our dataset, the Private Archive of Scanned Music Artifacts, or PASMA, which we reconstructed from the liner notes of 1,122 Taiwanese records released between 1957 and 1991. [指圖] 沿管線圖由上而下走。 An LLM pipeline works through five stages, from OCR correction to role classification. Since these historical credits have no external ground truth, a domain expert traces anomalies back to the source records. The result is a network of 3,688 people and institutions, linked whenever they share an album credit.

35 s Turning to the results, we begin with the collaboration network: when did it reorganise? [指圖] 由左到右 a、b、c；講 "military clubs" 指 b 右側新群。 Before 1976 the network is fragmented, with record labels at the centre, and around 1978 a new cohort appears: musicians from the American military clubs. By 1991 it has become a single giant component, with recording studios at the centre. Three metrics capture this change: clustering falls, the largest component absorbs almost everyone, and modularity drops. They are mathematically distinct properties, yet they turn in the same window.

36 s Who, then, holds the network together? A bridge is an actor whose ties span otherwise separate groups, and we measure this with Burt's structural-hole constraint. [指圖] 講 "vocalists" 指表格最後一列。 Vocalists are under-represented in the first two periods, and absent from the top 30 by 1991. Before 1976 the lyricists lead, but after 1978 the bridges are arrangers, session players, backing vocalists and engineers. Singing stars, in other words, were never the connective tissue of this industry, and the front-stage ontology of the historiography is inverted in the data.

7 s The strongest bridge across 35 years is the arranger CHEN Chih-yuan, with 178 albums and 338 collaborators.

38 s Several historical factors lie behind the timing of this turn, and the main one is technological. This is when Taiwan's industry adopted multitrack recording and made it the standard. The sparse network before 1978 is therefore more than missing data, because those roles could not yet claim a name on the sleeve. In SCOT terms, the technology and the social group that ran it stabilised together, which is what Pinch and Bijker call closure. Being named was itself a historical achievement; the full account is on our website.

35 s Then the sound: does it answer our question? The acoustic side is a separate corpus of 960 recordings, identified through the Taiwan Popular Music Database by arranger and year, from which we remove the vocals with Hybrid Demucs. This step is a methodological commitment: > "we listen to what the backstage workers made, not to the stars who sang over it." We then embed the instrumental stems with MERT and project them with UMAP. The colours on the following plots are applied after embedding.

33 s If people who work together sound alike, the turn in the network should be audible too. [指圖] 講 "distinct regions" 左右各指一下。 Each point is one recording, nearby points sound alike to the model, and the colour, before or after 1978, is added afterwards. The two periods occupy largely distinct regions, the divide we saw in the network, although the model was never told about 1978. What many listeners can tell by ear, roughly when a song was recorded, the embedding separates too.

48 s Next we ask whether it can hear the arranger, so here the same points are coloured by four arrangement traditions, which we defined from network communities before any listening. [指圖] 講 "spread across" 指 3b 的橘點；講 "recomputed" 指 3c。 Three traditions occupy their own regions, while the works of CHEN Chih-yuan, who worked across every tradition, spread across the whole space. When we recompute the projection without his works and his protégé's, the remaining traditions separate more sharply. We read this as an indication that social position and sound are entangled: what some listeners can tell, whose arranging it is, the embedding indicates too. This indicates that people who often made music together sound alike, though our traditions rest on arrangers alone.

32 s We make three contributions: a position, a dataset and a convergence test. We also see four limitations. The first three are practical: the acoustic evidence is still visual, validation concentrated on high-degree nodes, and the data is available only on request. The fourth, and most important to us, the intervention is partial: players before 1978 are present in the sound, yet still unnamed in the data, and our ongoing work aims to name them.

49 s Let us return to the restaurant, because what the listener heard there was real knowledge about real people. Around 1978 the network and the sound agree, which indicates that the reorganisation of backstage labour left traces in the sound itself. Born asks for agonistic interdisciplinarity, in which each side changes the other. Here, musicology changed what MIR labels, since traditions are defined by who worked together. And computation changed the history, since it dates the turn to a two-year window and finds one arranger across every tradition. If MIR is to diversify its objects and its methods, it must be willing to ask not only what algorithms can optimise, but what histories they can restore.
